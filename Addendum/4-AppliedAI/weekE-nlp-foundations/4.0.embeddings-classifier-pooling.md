# Week E — Day 4: From Word Vectors to Document Vectors — Pooling & Pretrained Embeddings
> Pairs with `4.1.embeddings-classifier-pooling.py`

## 📚 Topics
The aggregation problem (docs have many words, models want one vector), mean/max pooling, TF-IDF-weighted
pooling, pretrained embeddings (GloVe/word2vec downloads) = your first taste of TRANSFER LEARNING,
embeddings-vs-TFIDF bake-off, the information lost by pooling (word order!) → motivates Day 5.

## 🎯 Learning Goals
1. Frame the problem: [n_words, 300] → need [300]; every aggregation choice destroys something — name what
   (mean: emphasis; max: everything but peaks; all pooling: ORDER).
2. Use pretrained GloVe vectors: load, handle OOV (zeros? skip? UNK average? — test all three), build doc vectors.
3. Articulate why pretrained > self-trained here: 6B-token corpora encode similarity your 10k docs can't —
   transfer learning's core logic, stated a week before Week G makes it visual.
4. Run the bake-off under the Week-8 protocol: TF-IDF+LogReg vs mean-GloVe+LogReg vs TF-IDF-weighted-GloVe+LogReg.
5. Predict, then verify, WHERE embeddings win (short docs, synonym-heavy, small training sets) and where TF-IDF
   holds (long docs, topic keywords, enough data).

## 🛠️ Build in the paired .py
1. GloVe loader (100-d) + coverage report: % of your vocab found; what's missing (typos, rare words, names)?
2. Three pooling strategies as sklearn-compatible transformers (fit/transform — reuse your Week-4 custom-transformer skill).
3. The bake-off table: 3 representations × same classifier, same folds, mean ± std; bold the winner per dataset size
   (run at 100%/10%/1% of training data — the small-data row is where embeddings shine).
4. Failure inspection: 5 documents TF-IDF gets right and embeddings get wrong; read them; name the pattern (usually
   keyword-specific topics pooled into mush).
5. The order-blindness demo: "dog bites man" vs "man bites dog" → identical mean-pooled vectors. Print both. Stare.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes
OOV handled silently as zeros without measuring coverage first · comparing representations with different
classifiers (control!) · concluding "embeddings are better" from one dataset size.
