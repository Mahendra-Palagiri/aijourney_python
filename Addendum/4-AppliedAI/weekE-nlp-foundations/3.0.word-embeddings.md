# Week E — Day 3: Word Embeddings — Meaning as Geometry
> Pairs with `3.1.word-embeddings.py` · The conceptual hinge of the whole module.

## 📚 Topics
Distributional hypothesis ("you shall know a word by the company it keeps"), word2vec's two tricks
(skip-gram, negative sampling) at mechanics level, embedding spaces, cosine similarity (Week D-4 pays off),
analogies, bias in embeddings, GloVe in one paragraph.

## 🎯 Learning Goals
1. State what an embedding IS: a learned dense vector (50–300 dims) where geometric closeness ≈ semantic
   similarity — versus the one-hot/BoW world where every word is equidistant.
2. Explain skip-gram training mechanically: predict context words from center word; the "prediction layer"
   weights ARE the embeddings; loss = the same log-loss you've known since Week 5.
3. Explain negative sampling as "turn a 50k-way softmax into a few binary problems" (efficiency hack, same gradients in spirit).
4. Compute word similarity and the famous analogies (king − man + woman ≈ queen) and ALSO show where analogies fail
   (they fail often — honesty first).
5. Measure embedding bias (e.g., profession-gender cosine gaps) and connect to Week B Day 5: bias in data → bias in geometry.

## 🛠️ Build in the paired .py
1. Train word2vec on 20newsgroups (gensim, ~3 lines) — then IGNORE the magic and inspect: vocab, vector shapes, raw numbers.
2. `most_similar` by hand: cosine similarity over the whole matrix with YOUR Week-D function (no gensim helper) — match gensim's output.
3. The analogy function from scratch (vector arithmetic + nearest-neighbor excluding inputs); test 10 analogies, tabulate hits/misses honestly.
4. UMAP the top-300 words (Week C-6 discipline: neighborhoods only!); annotate 3 tight clusters you can name.
5. Bias probe: cosine(profession, "he") − cosine(profession, "she") for 20 professions; bar chart; one-paragraph reflection.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes
Expecting small-corpus embeddings to be good (they'll be mediocre — note it, it motivates pretraining/transfer, the
Module-4 macro-theme) · reading analogy parlor tricks as deep understanding · forgetting cosine ≠ Euclidean (normalize!).
