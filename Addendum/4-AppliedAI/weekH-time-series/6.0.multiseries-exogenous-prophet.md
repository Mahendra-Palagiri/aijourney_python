# Week H — Day 6: The Wider World — Multiple Series, Exogenous Variables, Prophet
> Pairs with `6.1.multiseries-exogenous-prophet.py`

## 📚 Topics
Forecasting MANY series at once (the retail/ops reality), global vs local models, exogenous regressors
(holidays, prices, weather), Prophet as the pragmatic decomposition-regression (and its limits), hierarchical
intuition (store→region→total must add up), anomalies in time series (Week C-7 returns with a time axis).

## 🎯 Learning Goals
1. Contrast local (one model per series — Day 1–5's world) vs global (one model, series-id as a feature,
   cross-learning) strategies; when does global win? (many short related series — the data-pooling argument.)
2. Add exogenous features properly: KNOWN-in-advance (holidays, planned promotions) vs only-observed
   (weather) — the forecasting-time availability question that decides what's legal at horizon h.
3. Run Prophet on one messy series: trend changepoints + seasonalities + holidays as a fitted GAM-ish
   decomposition; read its components plot with Day-1 eyes; know its reputation honestly (great intervals UI,
   middling accuracy — verify against your table).
4. Understand reconciliation at intuition level: independent forecasts of parts won't sum to the whole;
   bottom-up vs top-down as the simple fixes.
5. Detect anomalies via forecasting: |actual − forecast| > k·interval = flag (Week C-7's "anomaly = what the
   model didn't expect", now with temporal structure) — this IS Module 5-K's drift-monitor seed; plant it deliberately.

## 🛠️ Build in the paired .py
1. Get a multi-series dataset (M5 sample or synthetic store×item); global LGBM (series-id categorical) vs
   per-series ARIMA: aggregate MAE + per-series win/loss count.
2. Exogenous holidays into the LGBM features; quantify the lift on holiday weeks specifically (sliced eval — Week G-4 habit).
3. Prophet run + components plot + join the league table.
4. The reconciliation demo: store-level forecasts summed vs total-level forecast — show the mismatch, fix bottom-up.
5. The forecast-residual anomaly detector on one series with injected shocks; precision@k vs Week C-7's IsolationForest on the same task.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes
Exogenous features that won't exist at forecast time (deployment-day surprise) · global model without
per-series normalization (big series dominate the loss) · Prophet defaults trusted blindly (changepoint
flexibility is a real overfitting dial).
