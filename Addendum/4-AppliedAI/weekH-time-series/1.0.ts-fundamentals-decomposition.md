# Week H — Day 1: Time Series Fundamentals & Decomposition
> Pairs with `1.1.ts-fundamentals-decomposition.py`

## 📚 Topics
What makes time series different (order IS the information; observations are NOT iid — the assumption
behind most of weeks 3–8 quietly breaks), trend/seasonality/residual decomposition, additive vs
multiplicative, pandas time-series tooling (DatetimeIndex, resample, rolling, shift).

## 🎯 Learning Goals
1. Name the rupture explicitly: iid is dead; yesterday predicts today; everything from CV to train/test
   splitting must be rethought (Week 7's TimeSeriesSplit foreshadowed this — now it's home turf).
2. Drive pandas' ts toolkit fluently: parse dates as index, resample (D→W→M), rolling windows, shift/diff —
   the five verbs of time-series wrangling.
3. Decompose a series: trend (rolling mean or LOESS) + seasonality (period averages) + residual; choose
   additive vs multiplicative by whether seasonal amplitude grows with level (plot it; decide; justify).
4. Build the decomposition MANUALLY first (rolling mean → detrend → seasonal means → residual), then
   verify against statsmodels seasonal_decompose — the Week-6 pattern (scratch → library).
5. Read the residual like Week 6's residual plots: leftover structure = information your decomposition missed.

## 🛠️ Build in the paired .py
1. Load a classic seasonal dataset (AirPassengers or similar CSV — cache it, Week-8 style); the four-panel
   decomposition figure (observed/trend/seasonal/residual), built by hand.

3. The resample tour: same series at D/W/M with rolling means overlaid — what each timescale reveals/hides.
4. Calendar features: extract dayofweek/month/year; groupby-plot to SHOW the seasonality numerically (Week-2 groupby, new purpose).
5. shift()/diff() drills: lag-1 series, period-over-period change, percent change — the verbs of Day 3's features.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes
Decomposing before LOOKING at the raw plot · multiplicative data fed to additive decomposition (residual
inherits the seasonality — check it) · rolling windows that peek forward (center=True is for analysis, not features!).
