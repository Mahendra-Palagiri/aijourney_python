# Week D — Day 1: Probability & Distributions — The Vocabulary of Uncertainty
> Pairs with `1.1.probability-distributions.py` · Week-9-style: prose + tiny simulations, no heavy formalism.

## 📚 Topics
Random variables, PMF vs PDF vs CDF, expectation/variance as operators, the working zoo
(Bernoulli, Binomial, Poisson, Uniform, Normal, Exponential), joint/conditional/independence.

## 🎯 Learning Goals
1. Define a random variable as "a number generated by a random process" and a distribution as its full behavior catalog.
2. Explain PMF vs PDF correctly (PDF is density, P(X=x)=0 for continuous; only intervals have probability).
3. Compute E[X] and Var(X) for small discrete cases by hand; state linearity of expectation and why it needs NO independence.
4. Match each zoo member to its generating story (Bernoulli: one yes/no; Binomial: n independent yes/nos;
   Poisson: counts of rare events per window; Normal: sums of many small effects — foreshadows Day 2).
5. Write conditional probability P(A|B) = P(A∩B)/P(B) and recognize independence as "conditioning changes nothing".

## 🔍 Core Mechanics — connect everything backwards
- Week 5's logistic output IS a Bernoulli parameter p per row; log-loss IS the Bernoulli negative log-likelihood —
  derive this in 4 lines and feel weeks 5 and D-1 click together.
- Week 2's mean/std were ESTIMATES of E[X], √Var(X) — sample vs population distinction now made explicit.
- Histograms (Week 2) are empirical PMFs/PDFs; ECDF is often better — build one today.

## 🛠️ Build in the paired .py
1. Simulate each zoo member (`np.random`), overlay histogram with scipy.stats PDF/PMF — six-panel figure.
2. Verify E and Var numerically vs formula for Binomial(n=20, p=0.3) and Poisson(λ=4).
3. Linearity of expectation demo with DEPENDENT variables (X, X² say) — expectation still adds, variance does not.
4. Derive + verify: mean of Bernoulli sample = MLE of p (the proportion — you've used this forever, now name it).
5. ECDF function from scratch; plot against the true CDF for the Normal.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes
Density > 1 panic (it's fine, it's not probability) · variance of sum = sum of variances without independence ·
confusing the data distribution with the SAMPLING distribution of a statistic (Day 2's whole point).
