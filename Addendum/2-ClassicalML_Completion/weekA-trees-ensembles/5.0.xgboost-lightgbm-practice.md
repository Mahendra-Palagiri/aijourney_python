## 📚 Topics
What XGBoost adds over vanilla GBM (regularized objective, second-order gradients, column/row subsampling,
clever splits), LightGBM's histogram + leaf-wise growth, practical tuning recipe, early stopping.

## 🎯 Learning Goals
1. Name XGBoost's additions: L1/L2 penalty on leaf weights inside the objective, 2nd-order Taylor
   (uses hessian, not just gradient), min_child_weight/gamma as pruning-by-objective.
2. Explain LightGBM's two speed tricks: histogram binning of features; leaf-wise (vs level-wise) growth — and the
   overfitting risk leaf-wise brings on small data.
3. Run early stopping correctly: eval_set = validation NOT test; best_iteration; why this is just Week-7 honesty.
4. Apply the standard tuning order: n_estimators(+early stop) → max_depth/num_leaves → min_child_weight →
   subsample/colsample → learning_rate ↓ + retune budget → regularization (lambda/alpha).
5. Know when NOT to bother: tiny data (LogReg fine), strong linear signal, latency-critical scoring.

## 🔍 Core Mechanics
- XGBoost objective: Σ l(yᵢ, Fᵢ) + Σ [γ·T + ½λ‖w‖²] per tree — the split gain formula has λ and γ INSIDE it,
  so regularization shapes tree growth, not just final weights.
- Leaf weight closed form: w* = −G/(H+λ) where G,H = sums of gradients/hessians in the leaf. Connect: for
  squared loss H=1 and this reduces to the mean of residuals — Day 4's hand-rolled GBM is the λ=0 special case.
- Histogram trick: bin features into 255 buckets → split scan is O(bins) not O(rows).

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes
- Early stopping against the test set (most common leak in the wild).
- Copying Kaggle hyperparameters into a 900-row dataset (num_leaves=255 on Titanic = memorization).
- Comparing models with different effective budgets and calling the bigger one "better".
