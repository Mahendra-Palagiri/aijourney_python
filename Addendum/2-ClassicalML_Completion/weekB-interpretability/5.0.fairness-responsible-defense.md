# Week B — Day 5: Fairness, Responsible AI & the Upgraded Defense
> Pairs with `5.1.fairness-responsible-defense.py`

## 📚 Topics
Group fairness metrics, the impossibility trade-offs, proxy features, dataset bias, documentation
(model cards), and folding all of Week B into the Week-A defense.

## 🎯 Learning Goals
1. Compute group-wise metrics (selection rate, TPR, FPR per group) and three fairness notions:
   demographic parity, equalized odds, predictive parity.
2. Understand (at intuition level) why these can't all hold at once when base rates differ — work one
   numeric example, no proofs needed.
3. Hunt proxies: drop the sensitive feature, show the model reconstructs it from correlates (predict `sex`
   from other Titanic features — it works, that's the lesson).
4. Write a short model card for the Week-A model (intended use, data, metrics overall AND per group, limits).
5. Append an "Interpretability & Fairness" section to `weekA_defense.md` using Days 1–4 artifacts.

## 🔍 Core Mechanics
- Fairness metrics are just confusion-matrix arithmetic per group — you've owned these since Week 3/5;
  the new step is conditioning on a group and COMPARING.
- Removing the sensitive column ≠ fairness ("fairness through unawareness" fails via proxies) — your
  proxy-prediction experiment is the proof.
- Titanic note: `sex` drives survival because of historical evacuation policy — a clean case study in
  "the model learned the world as it was; is deploying that acceptable?" There is no math answer; the
  model card is where the judgment gets DOCUMENTED.

## 🛠️ Build in the paired .py
1. `fairness_report(model, X, y, group_col)` → per-group selection rate, TPR, FPR + the three parity gaps.
2. The proxy experiment (predict the sensitive feature from the rest; report AUC).
3. SHAP beeswarm sliced by group: does the model use different features for different groups?
4. The model card (markdown, ~1 page) + the defense addendum. These two documents are the day's real output.

## ⚠️ Common Mistakes
Computing fairness on train data · treating one parity metric as "the" definition · model card written as
marketing instead of limits-first.
